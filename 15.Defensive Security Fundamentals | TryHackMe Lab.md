## 1. Objective

Understand the role of defensive security and how organisations protect their infrastructure, data, and users.

This room introduced the Blue Team mindset, the importance of visibility across client infrastructure, and the relationship between prevention, detection, mitigation, and risk.

## 2. Key Concepts

### Blue Team

The Blue Team is responsible for defending systems, monitoring activity, investigating suspicious behaviour, and responding to security threats.

### Client Infrastructure

An organisation's client infrastructure includes its devices, networks, servers, and applications. Understanding these assets helps defenders identify what needs protection and where security controls should be applied.

### Visibility

Visibility is the ability to observe activity across systems and networks. Logs, monitoring tools, and security alerts help defenders identify unusual behaviour and investigate potential threats.

### Prevention, Detection, and Mitigation

- **Prevention:** Reduces the likelihood of an attack succeeding through controls such as access restrictions, secure configuration, and patching.
- **Detection:** Identifies suspicious activity using logs, alerts, and monitoring.
- **Mitigation:** Limits the impact of a threat once it has been identified, for example by isolating a compromised endpoint.

These functions complement one another. Prevention alone cannot stop every attack, so effective defence also requires detection and response.

### Threat and Risk

A **threat** is a potential source of harm, such as malware or a malicious actor.

**Risk** considers both the likelihood of a harmful event and its potential impact on an organisation.

## 3. Findings

- Defensive security focuses on protecting systems and responding to threats.
- Understanding an organisation's infrastructure helps identify assets that require protection.
- Visibility is essential for detecting suspicious activity and investigating security events.
- Prevention, detection, and mitigation serve different but complementary purposes.
- Thinking like an attacker helps defenders anticipate weaknesses and improve security controls.

## 4. Practical Security Relevance

These concepts provide a foundation for common Blue Team activities:

- Monitoring security alerts and system logs.
- Investigating unusual authentication activity.
- Identifying potentially compromised devices.
- Applying access controls and secure configurations.
- Containing threats and reducing their impact.

The room introduced these concepts at a foundational level; it did not constitute a full incident-response investigation.

## 5. Career Relevance

Defensive security knowledge supports further learning in several areas:

- **Security Operations Center (SOC):** Monitoring alerts, analysing logs, and investigating suspicious activity.
- **Threat Intelligence:** Researching threat actors, attack methods, and emerging threats.
- **Digital Forensics and Incident Response (DFIR):** Analysing security incidents, containing threats, and supporting recovery.
- **Cloud Security:** Protecting cloud infrastructure, identities, configurations, and workloads.

## Key Takeaway

Effective defensive security requires visibility into infrastructure, an understanding of potential threats, and a combination of prevention, detection, and mitigation. These fundamentals provide a starting point for progressing towards SOC analysis and broader cybersecurity roles.
